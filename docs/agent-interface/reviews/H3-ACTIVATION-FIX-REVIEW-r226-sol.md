## R226-1 — blocking: cleanup-task cancellation erases acquisition responsibility

`service/entrypoint.py:326–332` awaits acquisition/preparation without shielding inside cleanup, catches `BaseException` (including `CancelledError`), and then continues. If acquisition is cancelled at this await, its `to_thread` callable continues, but cleanup has no returned owner. Lines 333–342 consequently skip owner.close, close the notification and remove the global record. Strong retention does not prevent cancellation propagation into the awaited task.

Shielding the lifespan caller's wait at line 347 handles repeated cancellation of that caller. It does not protect the cleanup task when an event-loop shutdown sweep cancels pending tasks directly. This is a source responsibility-loss defect before loop death, not a claim that source can guarantee cleanup after the loop/process has died.

Independent inert probe used the existing real disposable ActivationTests Core/registration fixture. A Dispatcher wrapper constructed and captured the real owner, then blocked its return on a threading event. After startup cancellation entered cleanup, a second startup cancellation returned the caller while cleanup remained pending. The probe then cancelled `app.state.installed_lifecycle['task']` directly, modelling cancellation of that pending task by a shutdown sweep. Cleanup completed normally, before releasing the acquisition wrapper. After release the observed result was:

`notification_closed=True; owner_alive=True; tracked_owner=False; global_retained=False; error=CancelledError`.

The probe explicitly closed its captured live Dispatcher afterward through the ordinary close path and cleaned only its own disposable fixtures. No external job, listener or process inspection was involved. The preparation branch has the same catch-and-continue structure; its direct-task-cancellation consequence is a source observation, not a separately executed probe.

Required correction: distinguish successful completion/failure from cancellation of a still-running thread operation. Never interpret cancellation of its asyncio wrapper as completion of preparation/acquisition, and never close the notification or drop responsibility on that basis. Keep an independently retained way to observe the eventual thread result and acquired owner, including when cleanup itself is cancelled. Preserve failed/unknown obligations without a cancellation busy loop or unbounded retry. Add direct cleanup-task cancellation regression(s), alongside the existing repeated caller-cancellation tests. Abrupt loop/process termination must remain explicitly unqualified; no death/reap success may be inferred from it.
